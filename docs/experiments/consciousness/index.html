What this is

A systematic analysis of theories of consciousness — what gives rise to subjective experience — assessed along three dimensions:

  • Logical structure: Each theory's argument reconstructed as premises, inferences, and conclusions, with a scope line showing which assumptions are active at each step
  • Testability: Which claims within each theory can be tested with neuroscience, psychology, or physics — and which are purely metaphysical
  • Evidence: What the empirical data actually shows — including the 2025 Cogitate Consortium adversarial collaboration that directly tested the two leading theories

🔬 Landmark: The Cogitate Consortium (2025)

The first large-scale, preregistered adversarial collaboration directly comparing Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) — the two leading empirically-testable theories of consciousness.

🟡 IIT: Posterior cortex contains conscious content (partially confirmed), BUT sustained synchronization within posterior cortex was NOT found (key prediction failed), and maximum integrated information in posterior cortex was NOT confirmed.
🔴 GNWT: Lack of ignition at stimulus offset (challenged), limited prefrontal representation of conscious content (challenged), global broadcasting inconclusive.
📊 Methods: fMRI, MEG, and intracranial EEG across 256 participants. Preregistered predictions with pass/fail criteria agreed ex-ante by both theory proponents.

Cogitate Consortium, Ferrante et al. (2025). Nature, 642, 133–142. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1

"Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent." — Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, §7

Our guiding principle: theories that make no testable predictions are not wrong — they are not in the game. We catalogue them, but our energy goes where evidence can reach.

Scope Lines

Each theory's argument is reconstructed using scope line notation from formal proof theory. A scope line shows, for each step of an argument, which premises are still active assumptions. The format:

Step | Formula | Dependencies | Inference Rule | Active Assumptions

This makes visible which assumptions each conclusion depends on — and therefore which assumptions, if wrong, would collapse the argument.

Testability Tiers

  • Strongly testable: Makes quantitative, falsifiable predictions testable with current neuroimaging/physics technology
  • Weakly testable: Makes predictions that are testable in principle and partially testable now, but core claims remain difficult to access empirically
  • Testable in principle: Makes predictions that could be tested with foreseeable technology but have not yet been adequately tested
  • Not testable (metaphysical): Makes no empirical predictions. No conceivable observation could confirm or disconfirm the theory
